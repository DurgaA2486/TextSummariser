Alice says she has been to a day-school where they learned French and music. The Mock Turtle believes his school had extras like these but not washing, which made it better than Alice's school. The Mock Turtle teaches Reeling and Writhing and the different branches of Arithmetic, such as Ambition, Distraction, Ugliﬁcation, and Derision. He also attended a class called Mystery with subjects like Seaography and Drawling. When asked about his education, the Mock Turtle compares it to Alice's but admits it was better. The Gryphon is initially defensive when questioned; however, he later explains that they had lessons for ten hours a day, which he jokingly referred to as 'lessons' because they got shorter from day to day.

Alice had an unfamiliar encounter with a Mock Turtle and a Gryphon who asked her to join on a journey, emphasizing that "we don't want you with us!" The conversation centered around the importance of companions. Alice explained how a fish might ask about purpose instead of porpoise, but maintained she meant porpoise.

Alice tipped over the jury-box in haste causing some jurors to lay sprawled on the heads of the crowd below. She apologized profusely as she picked them up again, trying to regain her composure. The King asked Alice what business she knew about the proceedings and eventually an argument ensued between the King and the White Rabbit over what was "important" and what wasn't.

The Mock Turtle began to sing "Turtle Soup" from "The Lobster-Quadrille," but the Gryphon interrupted, asking Alice if she'd like a song instead. When she asked for the Mock Turtle's song, he persisted, asking about his toes and how he could turn them out with his nose during dancing.

Alice woke up from her long sleep with her sister gently brushing away dead leaves from her face. She told her sister about her strange dream, recounting her adventures in Wonderland. As she finished speaking, the creatures from her dream suddenly appeared before her again, accompanied by the sounds of a farmyard.

The Knave claims he did not sign a document with his name at the end, making it impossible to prove his guilt. The King believes this makes things worse thinking the Knave was up to mischief and should have signed like an honest man.

Alice questioned the meaning of the tarts, saying she doesn’t believe there is any actual truth in it. The jury writes down their verdict but the Queen insists on hearing the sentence first. When Alice disagrees with this the Queen orders her to be beheaded.

The King sharpily instructs a defendant, the Hatter, to give evidence regarding who stole the tarts. The Hatter, citing his "poor man" condition and recent tea consumption, claims that the twinkling of the tea and its taste led him to cut more bread-and-butter, which was too thin.

A whiting approached Alice, eager for her to join a dance called Lobster-Quadrille along the seashore. The creatures form two lines following certain rules before advancing twice, setting up partners, changing lobsters, and retreating in order. 

The Gryphon elaborated that some things are just done with certain substances like whiting for boots and shoes underwater. Upon being asked about shoemaking materials, Alice realized she didn’t know how to respond properly on her own.

Alice sat down with the Gryphon and Mock Turtle to learn about their Lobster Quadrille dance but felt puzzled by the whole thing.

The trial of the Knave of Hearts is underway in Wonderland's court. The Knave claims he did not sign a document with his name at the end, making it impossible to prove his guilt.

Alice was taken into custody along with other players at a croquet game, except for the King, Queen, and herself.
